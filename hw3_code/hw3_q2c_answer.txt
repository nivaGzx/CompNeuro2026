3. Answers to Problem 2(c) Questions:
   (a) Which PRC is Type I and which is Type II?
       - LIF neuron has a TYPE I PRC:
         R(phi) >= 0 everywhere over the cycle. Any depolarizing kick
         moves the voltage closer to threshold, strictly advancing the next spike.
       - FitzHugh-Nagumo oscillator has a TYPE II PRC:
         R(phi) is biphasic, possessing a substantial negative lobe (R < 0)
         as well as a positive lobe (R > 0).

   (b) What does a negative lobe mean physically?
       - Definition: A negative PRC value (R(phi) < 0) means that a depolarizing
         perturbation causes a PHASE DELAY (the next spike fires LATER than
         unperturbed, T' > Delta).
       - Biophysical Mechanism in FHN:
         In the FitzHugh-Nagumo model, during the downstroke and refractory phase
         (phi in [0.225, 0.788]), the slow recovery variable w is elevated.
         An extra depolarizing kick in v slightly increases v, but because
         dw/dt = 0.08*(v + 0.7 - 0.8*w), it accelerates the production of outward
         recovery variable w. The increased outward current deepens and prolongs
         the hyperpolarization valley, trapping the state longer before it can
         initiate the next upstroke. Hence, excitation paradoxically delays firing!
